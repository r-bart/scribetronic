# short-form-observation

A single noticed thing, stated clearly. The most disposable format AND the most viral one — because when an observation lands, it lands hard.

## Structure (X single)

```
[Observation as a single sentence. Declarative. Specific enough that the reader can imagine it.]

[Optional: 1-2 follow-up lines — the implication, the example, or the reframe.]
```

## Structure (LinkedIn — longer)

```
[The observation as the hook, 1-2 lines.]

[2-3 short paragraphs:
  - Where you noticed it.
  - Why it matters.
  - The tiny implication.]

[Closer: a one-liner, often the observation rephrased as a rule.]
```

## Rules

- **One observation per post.** Combining two splits the impact.
- **Specific > universal.** "Most newsletters die in month 3" beats "Newsletters are hard."
- **No teaching tone.** You're noticing, not instructing. Let the reader draw the lesson.
- **Earn the observation with detail.** _"Three of the four indie founders I talked to this month said the same thing about X."_ — vs _"I keep noticing that founders…"_.

## Anti-patterns

- "Just realized that…" — weak opener. Cut.
- Observation that's actually advice in disguise. If you're saying "you should X", it's not an observation, it's a how-to.
- Vague universals ("Life is hard", "People are weird"). The whole point is specificity.
- Adding a CTA. Observations don't sell — they earn trust.

## Example shapes

Three illustrative observation shapes (replace with examples in your own niche / voice):

- The reframed-pattern observation: _"Most people don't have a content problem. They have a thinking problem."_
- The metaphor-collapse observation: _"People don't follow dictionaries, they follow humans."_
- The honest-paradox observation: _"The week I shipped least was the week I learned most. That should bother me more than it does."_
